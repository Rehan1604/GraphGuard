---
title: Reviewing an alert
rules: general
---

## Evidence versus conclusions
- Evidence is what the data shows: which accounts, which amounts, which times. Keep it separate from your opinion about what it means.
- An alert describes a pattern that needs a human decision. It never proves that anyone did anything wrong.
- Write conclusions only after you have looked at the evidence, and say which evidence supports them.

## Documenting your review
- Record what you looked at, what you concluded and why, in plain sentences that a colleague could follow.
- Mention the innocent explanations you considered and whether the evidence supports or weakens them.
- Say clearly what you could not check, so the next reviewer does not assume it was covered.

## When to escalate
- Escalate when the evidence is hard to explain innocently and the decision belongs to someone with more authority or more information.
- Give the reason in your own words, so the person who receives the case does not have to start again.
- Escalating means asking for a closer look. It does not mean accusing anyone.

## Handling false positives
- Many alerts will turn out to be ordinary activity. That is expected and is the reason a human reviews them.
- When you dismiss an alert, write down why, so patterns in dismissals can be studied later.
- If similar harmless alerts keep appearing, report it so the thresholds can be reviewed.
