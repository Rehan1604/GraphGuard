## What the pattern is
- A transfer burst is an unusually large number of transfers involving one account inside a short time window.
- GraphGuard counts every transfer the account sends or receives and flags the account when the count passes a threshold.
- Bursts that involve only one or two other accounts look different from bursts spread across many accounts.

## Why it can matter
- Splitting one large movement into many small transfers can make each transfer look ordinary.
- A sudden change in an account's tempo compared with its history is often more informative than the raw count.
- The size of the burst above the threshold is a rough guide to how unusual it is.

## Innocent explanations
- A group dinner, event or trip where one person pays or collects many small amounts.
- A small business batching its daily payments or payouts.
- Subscription, rent or utility collections that all fall on the same day.
- An automated process that was re-run after a failure and sent the same payments again.

## What to check next
- Are the amounts similar to each other, or does each one have a different purpose?
- Is the account normally this busy, or is this a break from its usual pattern?
- Do the counterparties look related to each other or to the account holder?
- Check whether the same burst shape appeared on earlier days.
- Write down the evidence that supports or weakens each innocent explanation.
