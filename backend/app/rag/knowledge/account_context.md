---
title: Checking an account's own context
rules: general
---

## What to look at first
- Whether the account is a business or an individual. Businesses legitimately handle many counterparties and fast flows.
- How long the account has existed and what it normally does. A pattern that is routine for one account can be unusual for another.
- Whether the accounts in the alert have transacted with each other before. Repeat relationships are usually less surprising than first-time ones.

## Comparing with the account's usual behaviour
- Check typical amounts, typical timing and typical number of counterparties for the same account over earlier weeks.
- A pattern is more interesting when it is new for that account, larger than usual, or faster than usual.
- A pattern that repeats exactly every week or month is often a scheduled process.

## Looking at the surrounding activity
- Look at what else happened around the same accounts during the same hours. Alerts that cover most of the nearby activity look more deliberate than alerts buried in a lot of ordinary traffic.
- Look at where the money came from before the pattern and where it went afterwards.

## What not to conclude
- Account context can explain a pattern, but it can also be missing or wrong. If the context is unknown, say it is unknown instead of guessing.
- Do not infer anything about a person from an account type or an amount alone.
