title: Everyday money flows that look like patterns
rules: general
---

## Payroll and bulk payouts
- A business paying many employees or suppliers on the same day sends money out to many different accounts in a short time. This is the same shape as a many-counterparties alert.
- Clues that it is routine: the same account does it on a regular schedule, the amounts follow a pay structure, and the recipients are accounts that have been paid before.

## Marketplace and platform settlements
- A marketplace collects payments from buyers and pays sellers shortly afterwards, keeping a small fee. Money enters and almost all of it leaves quickly, which resembles a pass-through chain.
- Clues: the middle account is a platform, the fee is a consistent percentage, and the same pattern repeats across many sellers.

## Shared costs and group payments
- Friends or family splitting a bill, a trip or rent can move money around a small group so that each person ends up square. A loop of similar amounts can appear.
- Clues: small everyday amounts, people who transact with each other often, and a payment reason that fits.

## Refunds and reversals
- A refund returns money to where it started, so a payment followed by its refund can look like a circular flow.
- Clues: the amounts match closely, the refund follows a recent purchase, and the accounts have an ordinary buyer and seller relationship.

## Busy periods
- Sales events, rent day, tax deadlines and salary dates create bursts of activity on accounts that are otherwise quiet.
- Clues: many accounts show the same spike on the same day, and the amounts fit what that account normally handles.

## Why this matters for review
- Many alerts that match one of these descriptions are harmless. Recognising them quickly saves time for the alerts that need attention.
- Being a familiar kind of flow is not proof of innocence. The same shapes can also be used to hide something, so a reviewer still compares them with the account's own history.
